A special-topics course at the University of Cincinnati Blue Ash College, first offered in Spring 2027.

Generative AI is changing how creative work gets made and who gets to make it. In this hands-on course you will explore what these systems can actually do, including generate, edit, extend, restore, translate, and direct work, and how to choose the right tool for a creative problem. You will build an original project in which AI extends your own creativity rather than replacing it, document and defend the choices you made along the way, and work through the legal, ethical and labor questions now reshaping the industries you are entering. No prior experience required. Open to non-majors.

## What students will be able to do

1. Situate generative AI within the longer history of computational creativity, distinguishing where the two converge and where they diverge.
2. Evaluate the legal, ethical, and labor implications of AI use in creative production.
3. Differentiate the core capabilities of generative systems — generate, edit, extend, restore, translate, and direct — and select appropriate capabilities for a given creative problem.
4. Produce an original creative work in which generative AI augments rather than substitutes for the student's own authorship.
5. Document and justify AI use across a creative workflow, including iterative development and disclosure consistent with professional practice.
6. Critique AI-assisted creative work, one's own and others', using aesthetic criteria appropriate to the medium.

## How the course uses AI

The course has to teach capabilities that outlast the products that deliver them today — generating, editing, extending, restoring, translating, directing — and it has to put students' hands on those products anyway. It has to require documented AI use as assessed work while forbidding AI as a substitute for the work itself.

Applied Media Communications majors and non-majors produce real creative work with generative tools under per-assignment rules on permitted use, scheduled checkpoints, and two-way disclosure: students document their AI use as graded work, and the instructor documents his in the course materials.

## Access to tools

Grants I secured from ElevenLabs and Autodesk give students access to AI platforms that many would not be able to afford on their own, tools they would otherwise not have access to.

## Who can take it

The course counts as an elective in the AAS in Applied Media Communications, in both the standard program and the Animation track.
